What this plan bets on

The problem
Every number anyone quotes about Grüns is a marketing number. A million members, 95% adherence, 100,000 reviews. Not one of them tells you how many people are paying you this month, how many cancelled last month, or why.
The bet
Fix the funnel in order: capture, convert, retain, scale, refer. Nothing downstream gets funded until the layer above it is instrumented, and every dollar from week 5 is directed rather than guessed.
The cost
Month one's revenue and the Q4 window. You spend three weeks building and see almost nothing. That is not a side effect of this plan, it is the plan.
Subscription rule
Retain is not reorder. It is retention, churn and reactivation. That matters more here than anywhere, because the events that break are skip, pace change, pause, cancel and cancel-reason, and most Shopify subscription stacks fire those into nothing.

Growth outcome

Contribution margin per acquired cohort, tracked by acquisition week.

Revenue minus COGS, fulfilment, discount and acquisition cost. Chosen deliberately because you cannot calculate it today, and you will not be able to until the whole funnel is instrumented. The metric becoming computable is itself the first deliverable. Until then every channel decision is an opinion with a dashboard attached.

Week one

Three things, roughly nine hours in total. Nothing is sent. No campaign launches, no budget moves, no creative is briefed.

Days 1 to 3, 6h

Trace every subscription event.

With an engineer, across Klaviyo and your billing platform. Created, renewed, skipped, paced, paused, cancelled, reactivated. Write down which arrive, which arrive late, and which do not exist. That list is the plan.

Days 3 to 5, 3h

Find your true reachable list.

Deliverability, engagement decay, real active size against nominal, SMS consent by cohort. Fair warning: the million-member number shrinks a lot here, and it should.

Days 6 to 7, 1h

Decide if you can stomach it.

Three weeks of no new spend during Q4 ramp is the real cost. If that is not survivable inside your organisation, say so now and switch, rather than discovering it in week 7.

Week one output. A ranked list of event gaps and the truth about your list size. Nothing else, and nothing sent. If week one produces a campaign, the plan has already failed, because the ranking that decides what to build has not happened yet.

Cancel-reason capture.

Five structured options at cancellation: too expensive, too much product, taste, stopped caring, switched brands, plus free text. This is the highest-value data you are not collecting. If your subscription platform cannot do it inside 10 days, fall back to a post-cancel email survey, which captures maybe 20% of the signal instead of 95%.

Your current capture is almost certainly one discount-for-email popup, which on a brand already running "up to 55% off" adds nothing and trains discount-seeking. Retain

Days 43 to 56, 2 plays
When someone is about to leave, do you know it, and do you know why?
9% monthly churn→10% save rate

Reason-matched saves.

SMS to pre-churn signals: skipped twice, paced down, opened the cancel page. Deliberately do not pick the angle yourself. The cancel-reason data built in week 2 picks it, which is the entire reason that work came first.

10h setupStop if under 10% save rate after 3,000 interventions; churn is being decided earlier and the work moves into onboarding

The 3/14/30 ladder.

Email and SMS to subscribers under 30 days. You already sell this ladder on the site, fewer cravings by day 3, energy by day 14, skin by day 30, and then never confirm it after purchase. Closing that loop is the cheapest retention win available to you. Ship it with a 10% holdout so you can prove it worked.

10h setup, ship with a 10% holdoutStop if no separation against holdout by day 70

Runs at the same tier as convert. Yobo Senior, $2,000/mo takes the saves, the ladder and the holdout reporting.

Scale

ROAS-ranked→margin-ranked

Reallocate on measured margin, not ROAS.

Rank every channel by the contribution margin of the cohort it produced. Expect this to reorder your priorities, possibly severely. Then scale exactly one channel 2x, so the read stays clean. One channel, not three, because three at once means you learn nothing from any of them.

This is the first stage where paid budget moves on evidence rather than instinct. Yobo Senior, $2,000/mo runs the reallocation and the single scale test.

Open it to subscribers past 90 days, three or more orders, zero skips. Nobody else. Referral before retention just distributes a leaky product faster, which is why it sits at the end of the sequence rather than the start, where most plans put it.

The rhythm

One window at a time. The gate in week 3 is the only place the plan is allowed to change shape.

Weeks 1 to 3

Capture. Audit, list truth, cancel reasons, on-site capture, segments. Nothing is sent and nothing is spent.

Week 3

Re-baseline. Every assumed number replaced with a measured one, in writing, and every later target revised against it.

Weeks 4 to 6

Convert. Welcome-to-subscribe, one-time to subscription, three on-site tests run in sequence.

Weeks 7 to 8

Retain. Reason-matched saves off real cancel data, and the 3/14/30 ladder with a holdout.

Weeks 9 to 11

Scale. Contribution margin per cohort is now reportable weekly. Reallocate, then double exactly one channel.

Weeks 12 to 13

Refer, if retention earned it. If it did not, the hours go back into onboarding instead.

The sequence is the product. Running two windows at once does not compress the plan, it removes the only thing that makes the later numbers trustworthy. If a window slips, the windows behind it slip with it.

Access on day 5

This plan is strictly sequential, so unlike a parallel plan there is nothing useful to do while you wait. If Klaviyo and billing platform access is not sorted by the end of week 1, the whole 90 days shifts, not just week 1.

Check at day 5. Stop and fix it before continuing.

The week-2 question

Someone will ask why nothing has shipped yet and why no Black Friday creative is running. That is a real signal about organisational patience, not impatience to be managed. If the answer is that the organisation cannot hold three quiet weeks, switch plans then, cleanly, rather than abandoning this one halfway.
