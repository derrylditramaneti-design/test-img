What this plan bets on

The problem
A funnel does not care what you are good at. It leaks where it leaks, and the leakiest stage caps every stage above it. Doubling acquisition into a funnel that loses people on day 12 just means paying twice to lose twice as many.
The bet
The binding constraint is not where you think it is, and finding it beats any single tactic.
The cost
Week one produces a diagnosis, not a campaign. You will change your own priorities twice, on purpose, at day 30 and day 60.
Subscription rule
Retention here means stopping a cancel, fixing the reason and winning back the lapsed. Never reorder, because a buy-again campaign competes with your own subscription. And activation means the first dose, not the first charge. A subscriber who bought and never opened the pouch reads as a win for four weeks, then churns and takes the acquisition cost with them.

Growth outcome

Revenue per 1,000 site visitors, over 90 days.

Every stage feeds this number and no stage can fake it. It goes down when one stage is optimised at the expense of another, which is exactly what a plan that moves budget between stages needs. Cheap traffic that never activates, or a discount that lifts conversion and eats margin, both show up here as a loss.

Retention

4 plays
Do they stay past the point where novelty ends and the charge is just a charge?
55% at day 90→64%

Intercept cancels within 24 hours.

Offer order matters: pace change first, skip second, discount last. Leading with a discount on a brand already anchored at "up to 55% off" teaches people to use the cancel page as a haggling tool.

4h setup, 1h/wkStop if save rate stays under 8% by day 21. The decision is being made earlier, so the work belongs in activation

Make pace change the default save.

SMS to anyone with two or more skips in 90 days. "You have skipped twice. Want every 6 weeks instead?" A skip is a cadence complaint, not a product one, and a subscriber at a slower pace is worth vastly more than a cancelled one at any pace.

3h setupStop if under 5% change pace. Then the signal is price, not cadence

Send the study at the moment of doubt.

Day 35 to 60, where the honeymoon ends and "is this actually doing anything?" starts. Not "buy this, it is proven" but "you have taken this five weeks, here is what is measurably happening."

8% attach→12%, price held

Diagnose household attach first.

One email to adult subscribers past 60 days with no Kids SKU: "Your kids keep stealing them." Cheap, fast, and it settles whether this is an awareness problem or a price problem before you spend real money finding out.

1hStop if CTR is under 1.5%, and move the budget to Sugar-Free instead

Then build the household bundle.

Adult plus Kids on one subscription, priced below the sum. The same consolidation motive that sold the original product, moved up a level. Household subscriptions churn structurally lower, so this is a revenue play that pays a retention dividend.

Do not ship if bundle margin falls below your blended subscription margin, even when conversion looks good

Test Sugar-Free as co-hero.

It currently sits as a priced-up option. In a GLP-1 and low-sugar era that hierarchy is backwards, and this is defensive as much as commercial.

Week one

One deliverable: every stage sized in dollars per month, ranked. Nothing launches before it exists. Roughly twelve hours in total.

Days 1 to 2, 2h

Get in and look.

Confirm access, start tracing events, check whether cancel reasons are captured at all. Most brands find they are not.

Days 3 to 5, 8h

Build the five numbers.

Then convert each one into dollars lost per month, because percentages hide big leaks on big volume.

Days 6 to 7, 2h

Rank and pick two.

Five stages ordered by revenue lost. The top two get week-two budget. Write it down, because you will revisit it twice.

The five numbers: visitor-to-subscriber conversion split by traffic source. The day-30 activation curve, and where inside days 0 to 30 people actually leave. Retention by acquisition cohort at day 30, 60 and 90. Kids attach rate. Blended realised price per pack.

Dollars, not percentages. A 3-point leak at activation on your volume beats a 15-point leak at referral. Sizing it that way needs COGS and fulfilment cost per unit. Without them we can rank leaks by conversion but not by money, which halves the value of the week.

Three things to watch

An operational answer

If the first-week sequence moves nothing on day-30 activation, the problem is that first shipments arrive late, and no email fixes that.

Watch at day 45. Escalate as operations, not marketing.

Re-ranking read as drift

Someone will ask in week 6 why you have changed direction. That is the predictable cost of a constraint-led plan. If the organisation reads it as indecision rather than discipline, a plan that commits in week one and holds suits you better.

Better to decide that at day 30 than day 75.

Discount dependency

With "up to 55% off" plus auto-applied discounts plus a permanent birthday rate, saves and attach lifts can both happen at prices that never earn back acquisition cost.
