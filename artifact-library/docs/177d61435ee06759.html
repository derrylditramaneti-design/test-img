What this plan bets on

DTC subscription supplements is one of the most thoroughly solved growth categories of the last decade, and the brands that solved it did it in public. This is not a set of hypotheses. It is a set of transplants.

The bet
This category is already solved. Copy what worked here rather than invent. Every play below names the brand that proved it, the published benchmark, and the gap you are closing.
The cost
Originality. You will always run one cycle behind whoever is inventing, and you accept that in exchange for knowing what good looks like before you spend.
The trap
A move that worked for AG1 in 2022 worked under 2022 CPMs and a category that did not exist yet. Several stop-conditions below fire on the condition, not the number.
Subscription rule
Every repeat benchmark here is retention, churn or reactivation. Many published DTC repeat-rates are transactional and not comparable, which is why the benchmarks are AG1, Ritual and Seed rather than retail-led brands.

How we label benchmarks

A plan built on other people's numbers only works if you can see which numbers are real. Three labels run through this file.

Published published means the brand or a named outlet stated it. Reported reported means a credible third party inferred it from ad libraries, site structure or category reporting. Assumed assumed means we estimated it and it has not been checked. Any assumed benchmark a play depends on gets verified in week one, before that play's budget is committed. If a number moves the plan, it does not stay assumed.

The rhythm

Three phases, one real gate. The gate is what makes this a benchmark plan rather than a list of copied tactics.

Week 1

Measure. Retention curve built, four competitor subscriptions started, legal path opened. Nothing launches and nothing is spent.

Days 1 to 30

Close the cheap gaps. Adherence claim, the 3/14/30 ladder, the study moved to the moment of doubt.

Days 31 to 60

The two expensive benchmarks. Creator volume restarts, comparison search goes live, winback ships off the competitor sequences now sitting in your inbox.

Week 8

Score every play against its benchmark. Anything under 60% of benchmark gets killed, not optimised.

Days 61 to 90

Household bundle, then put the remaining budget behind whatever cleared the cull.

Why kill rather than tune. A play failing to reach a number someone else already hit has a wrong premise, not a tuning problem. The benchmark says the number is reachable, so a miss means the transplant did not take. That rule is the entire discipline of this option, and it only works if you apply it on the date rather than after one more test.

Three things to watch

Whether the conditions still hold

Bloom's creator economics were bought in a cheaper market. A benchmark is a number plus the conditions that produced it, and only the number travels.

Track cost-per-creator-post against the historical figure. More than 2x means you are copying into conditions that already killed the move.

Whether AG1 notices

The comparison-search play is a direct attack inside their auction, and they have the budget to make it expensive rather than lose it.

Watch their bid density on those terms weekly. A sharp rise within 14 days is the stop signal regardless of CPA.

Whether the delta was ever real

Four of these benchmarks are measured against assumed Grüns numbers. If day-90 retention is actually 68%, you have built twelve weeks of work to close a gap that does not exist.

That is why week one is measurement with zero spend, and why the retention curve carries a stop condition.

Spend week one proving the gap is real, then close it with moves that already worked.
